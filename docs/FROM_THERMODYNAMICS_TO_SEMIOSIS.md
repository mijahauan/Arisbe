# From Thermodynamics to Semiosis

> **What this chapter holds.** The intellectual history behind one question:
> how does a structure that merely persists come to *use* something as standing
> for something else? Arisbe has assumed an answer to this question in every
> loop it has run, and has never written the assumption down. This records the
> lineage, marks where its authors disagree, and states what each disagreement
> would cost a model that tried to cross the line rather than start past it.
>
> Read after [THE_KYTOS.md](THE_KYTOS.md) (the membrane doctrine and the
> fractal anatomy) and beside
> [THE_LINEAGE_AND_THE_TRIBUTARIES.md](THE_LINEAGE_AND_THE_TRIBUTARIES.md),
> which tells the wider intellectual history as narrative. The one-line graded

## 1 · The question, and why it kept returning

Every loop this project has built begins already past the interesting part. A
unit holds a model, anticipates from it, meets the world, and revises. Grant it
those and the machinery follows. Withhold them and nothing starts.

So the question arrives from behind: what drives an interpretant to hold a
representation *for* anything? Something, once, for the first time, stood for
another thing in the service of a third. What conditions in the world at large
allowed that, and can we reconstruct them?

The trap sits right there. Each time the project meets a capacity it suspects
arose on its own, it installs the capacity and looks for the conditions
afterward. The C-series installed typification and then went hunting for a
world where typifying would pay. That inverts the order. Typification, on the
account this chapter assembles, exists *because* of such a world, and a system
handed the mechanism for free has no reason to develop, keep, or refine it.

The circle looks vicious: purpose needs representation, representation needs an
interpretant, an interpretant gets defined by purpose. It binds only under one
assumption — that purpose must itself be represented. The tradition below
denies that assumption, and the denial is where the chapter begins.

## 2 · The floor: persistence as a fact, not a purpose

**Schrödinger (1944).** *What is Life?* asked how an organism resists the decay
its own physics predicts, and answered that it feeds on a gradient — drawing
order from its surroundings rather than manufacturing it. The organism buys its
own improbability by making the world outside it more probable. Nothing in that
account mentions ends. It states a fact about which structures last.

**Prigogine (1960s–70s).** Dissipative structures made the fact general. Hold a
system far from equilibrium and pour energy through it, and structure appears
and maintains itself — the convection cell, the flame, the whirlpool — not
because anything wants it, but because that arrangement dissipates the gradient
and the alternatives do not. Order arrives through fluctuation, and it persists
for as long as the flow does.

This floor deserves stating carefully, because the whole argument rests on
refusing to over-read it. **A dissipative structure persists. That amounts to a
simple fact, in service of nothing.** It carries no goal, no norm, no interest.
The candle flame does not want to burn.

What the fact supplies is a **gradient**, and the gradient does the later work.
Some perturbations end such a structure and others leave it standing, and that
asymmetry exists whether or not anything registers it. Purpose does not live in
the floor. It becomes *possible* on the floor, because the floor makes some
differences matter in a way that needs no observer to certify.

**Lotka (1922)** pushed the fact toward selection: organisms that capture and
route more energy tend to prevail, so evolution acquires a thermodynamic
direction. **Morowitz** made the cycle the unit — energy flowing through a
system organizes that system into cycles. **Kauffman** gave the chemistry a
shape with autocatalytic sets, where a collection of molecules catalyzes its
own production, and the collection persists though no single molecule does.
**Rosen** put the same intuition in relational terms, asking what closure a
system must have to count as alive at all.

None of these authors reaches a sign. They establish the scaffold and its
gradient, and they leave a real gap above them.

## 3 · The transition, and the disagreement that matters

Above the floor the tradition splits, and the split repays attention, because
the candidates place the threshold in different places and each placement
demands different apparatus of anyone modelling it. Reading them as one
consensus would waste the most useful thing they offer.

### Pattee — the epistemic cut

Howard Pattee posed the physicist's version: how do rate-independent
constraints arise within, and come to govern, rate-dependent dynamics? A
sequence acts as a symbol only when its *shape* matters and its chemistry does
not, and something must read it. He named the boundary the epistemic cut — the
line separating a description from what it describes — and argued that no
account escapes drawing it somewhere.

*What this threshold demands of a model:* something that acts by its form
rather than by its force, and machinery that responds to that form.

### Bickhard — recursive self-maintenance

Mark Bickhard's interactivism supplies the sharpest candidate for the exact
transition this chapter chases, and it turns on one distinction.

A candle flame maintains the conditions of its own persistence: it melts wax,
draws oxygen, sustains its temperature. Call that **self-maintenant**. It has
no options. A bacterium swimming up a sugar gradient, and tumbling when the
gradient falls away, does something else — it *selects among ways of
maintaining itself* according to conditions. Call that **recursively
self-maintenant**.

That second system must somehow indicate which interaction suits the present
conditions, and the indication can be wrong: the anticipated interaction may
simply fail to go as indicated. Two consequences follow, and they matter more
than anything else in this chapter.

The indication carries a **truth condition**, because the world can fail to
oblige. And the system itself can **detect the failure**, because the
interaction it undertook does not proceed. Error becomes error *for the
system*, not for an observer scoring it from outside.

There, on this account, something first stands for something else in the
service of a third — where the third names neither a homunculus nor an
intention but the system's own continued existence, which the interaction
either serves or fails to.

*What this threshold demands of a model:* variability such that no single fixed
action always works; internal state that can covary with which action will
work; and interaction failure the system registers as failure.

### Deacon — teleodynamics and the autogen

Terrence Deacon's *Incomplete Nature* (2011) attacks the same seam by asking
how ends can matter physically when what an end names has not yet happened —
how absence can be causal. His proposed minimal crossing, the autogen, couples
two self-organizing processes so that each produces the conditions the other
needs: autocatalysis generates the components that self-assemble into a
container, and the container preserves the autocatalysis. Neither alone
persists; together they do, and the pair can be disrupted and reconstituted.

*What this threshold demands of a model:* reciprocal enabling between processes
that individually run down — persistence that belongs to the coupling rather
than to any part.

### Barbieri — the code

Marcello Barbieri's code biology puts the threshold elsewhere, at **arbitrary
mapping**. Sign-hood on his account requires a code — a correspondence not
fixed by chemistry, with something that maintains it — and he locates the first
one in the genetic code. On this reading the bacterium's gradient-following
does not yet count, because nothing there stands for anything arbitrarily.

*What this threshold demands of a model:* a mapping that could have gone
otherwise, and machinery holding it in place.

### Millikan — proper function

Ruth Millikan's teleosemantics fixes *content* by history: what a state
represents amounts to what it was selected for doing. That answer bears
directly on any population modelling, and it carries a sharp consequence. **On
this account there can be no representational content before a lineage.**
Reproduction stops being decoration on the experiment and becomes constitutive
of the thing the experiment hopes to observe.

*What this threshold demands of a model:* heritable variation and differential
reproduction — a population with a history, not an individual with a ledger.

## 4 · Where Peirce sits, and the tension worth keeping

The project takes Peirce as its ground, so an honest record has to name a
tension rather than smooth it.

Peirce held Thirdness irreducible. Genuine mediation does not decompose into
brute dyadic reaction, and no amount of Secondness sums to a sign. Read
strictly, that puts him against any story deriving semiosis bottom-up from
dynamics, which describes most of §3.

Two things ease the tension without dissolving it. Peirce's cosmology already
makes habit-taking a *tendency* rather than a finished fact — habits grow, and
the universe acquires them. And his later work makes the final interpretant a
**habit of action**, which places the terminus of semiosis in disposition
rather than in contemplation. A story that ends at "a system disposed to act
one way rather than another, and correctable when the world declines" lands
much closer to Peirce than its thermodynamic starting point suggests.

The residue stays real. Peirce would likely deny that the bacterium's switch
constitutes a genuine sign rather than a degenerate case, and this chapter does
not claim to have settled that against him. It records where the disagreement
falls.

## 5 · What the lineage asks of Arisbe

Read against the built system, the tradition returns an uncomfortable verdict,
and the verdict explains a pattern the C-series kept running into.

Arisbe's units satisfy the middle conditions and fail the outer ones. They
interact, their world varies, and they hold state that covaries with it. But
**nothing stands at stake for them.** They cannot die, they need not acquire
anything to continue, and a failed prediction costs them nothing —
`MembraneLedger` keeps an observer's scorecard, not a stake. Failure gets
detected by whoever reads the log, never by the unit in any way that bears on
its persistence.

By Bickhard's criterion these units never reach recursive self-maintenance,
because they were never self-maintenant to begin with. Nothing about their
continuation depends on anything they do.

That single absence accounts for three findings the project reached separately
and reported as unrelated. Nothing scarce leaves an economizing habit no
gradient to climb, so typification reads inert. Nothing mortal leaves no
maintenance cost, so West's exponent has nothing to measure. And nothing at
stake leaves no third party the sign could serve, so no strategy has any reason
to develop and each one has to get installed by hand. **One absence, three
symptoms** — and the third names the other two.

The pattern shows up in the measurements. Every C-series mechanism built in
abundance read inert or harmful; the ask channel paid only under bounded
attention, and the silence window — a bounded inquiry budget, the one genuinely
scarce resource in the design — became the only mechanism in the series that
ever discriminated on whether a law holds.

## 6 · The rule that keeps a reconstruction honest

The circularity turns vicious only in one specific way, and naming it precisely
gives the escape.

**Install the problem. Never install the solution.**

Positing the predicament does not beg the question — it sets the question.
Physics hands real organisms their stake for free; a simulation, having no
thermodynamics, must posit one, and that posit stays honest and unavoidable. It
differs in kind from positing the response. A genome carrying a `typify` switch
makes "typification evolved" mean only that selection flipped a bit somebody
installed. A genome carrying general capacities — allocate effort, retain
outcomes, condition action on what got retained — leaves typification as one
reachable strategy among many that were equally reachable, and finding it
becomes a result. Finding something else instead also becomes a result.

The standing falsifier follows the same logic and needs stating in advance,
because at this level the temptation to read structure into noise runs far
stronger than at any level the project has worked at so far.

**Ablate the putative sign.** Occlude it, randomize it, sever its covariance
with the world — and if the system performs as well without it, nothing there
stood for anything, whatever the correlations looked like. Arisbe has already
caught itself reading structure into noise three times with much simpler
instruments: seven two-supplier cases that turned out to be one fact arriving
twice, thirteen apparent choices that vanished when counted on fresh uptake,
and a gate whose stated cause proved wrong while its counts stayed right. No
claim that something stands for something else should stand here without an
ablation showing the standing-for does work.

## 7 · What remains open

The chapter records a lineage and settles nothing empirically. Four questions
stay live, and the first one governs the rest.

**Which threshold does a reconstruction aim at?** §3's candidates disagree, and
the disagreement decides the apparatus. Pattee's cut asks for form-driven
constraint; Bickhard's asks for interaction failure the system registers;
Deacon's asks for reciprocal enabling; Barbieri's asks for an arbitrary code;
Millikan's asks for a lineage. A design aimed at all five would build
everything and demonstrate nothing.

**Can a simulated stake do the work a thermodynamic one does?** The posit stays
honest, but a stipulated persistence condition may prove too thin to generate
what a real gradient generates. Nobody here knows yet.

**Does the ablation discipline survive contact with a population?** Three
catches at small scale give no assurance at large.

**And what would count as having crossed?** Stated before the first run, or the
crossing will get recognized in whatever the run happens to produce.
