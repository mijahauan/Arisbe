Dear Professor Pietarinen,

I have built an environment for existential graphs whose treatment of the
game rests directly on your formalization, and whose two most exposed
doctrinal choices run, respectively, with and against your published work.
That combination leaves me needing exactly the judgment the project cannot
render for itself, so I write to ask for it.

First the debt. The evaluation engine reads a graph endoporeutically — the
nest sucks the meaning from without inwards unto its centre — following your
account in *Signs of Logic* and the 2005 composition paper. We keep a
documented conformance check against that account: the correspondences, and
two standing divergences held openly rather than papered over. Your players
evaluate against a completed common model and never disagree; ours argue.
Whether that divergence deepens the game or breaks faith with it, you can
judge better than we can. And Peirce's remark that truth forms a habit "of a
tolerable stable nature" gets taken literally enough here to measure:
settlement stickiness, disuse-decay, and a meta-learning layer that asks
which ways of settling disputes produce knowledge that lasts.

Four claims, each stated so you can refute it:

1. **The Agonothetes.** Peirce's game terminates at a dyad; his semiotic
   runs triadic. We name the third, telic function — the interpretant of the
   game as semiosis — and since July we hold a sharpened role account: two
   players only, no move-by-move referee (legality belongs to the calculus),
   and the Agonothetes standing not as a player but as the maker of two
   risked choices — before the game, the choice of the model; after it, the
   selection of a fate from an agreed taxonomy. We believe this reading
   faithful to Peirce. We would like to hear if it is not.
2. **Modality without Gamma.** Arisbe draws no modal mark. The branching
   record itself serves as the frame: possibility where some legal
   trajectory scribes a graph, necessity where every one does. We know this
   runs against Ma & Pietarinen 2018, whose broken-cut calculi stand sound
   and complete; we claim adequacy for this architecture, not completeness,
   and we call the choice architectural rather than a refutation of Gamma.
   Does that positioning read as honest, or as an evasion?
3. **Mention-ascent.** Peirce's graph of a graph runs native here: a dotted
   oval as a quotation device carried in the core — a sorted name with a
   graph-valued area, mention never use, conservativity-gated — with
   hypostatic abstraction as the ascent operator. It yields a third tense we
   find ourselves using: a withdrawn law can remain on the sheet as exhibit,
   present without force.
4. **The question as a first-class object.** A verdict of UNKNOWN begins a
   career here rather than ending one: it mints a standing question that
   cites the evaluation that raised it, gets traced for the consequences of
   each answer, priced by an attention economy, and settled only by a
   licensed step the record can cite. We read this as the pragmatist account
   of doubt made operational — the question given the same citizenship as
   the assertion. You may read it otherwise, and we would learn from that.

Two further things stand behind the letter. Your community's manuscripts
render here through the real engine — MS 514, CP 4.394, the scroll drawn
with its self-continuing glyph — and export to publication-grade TikZ wedded
to the underlying graph, so a printed figure provably denotes what the
manuscript asserts; a reader can run the whole path with one command. And
for teaching: the game runs playable in a browser, a challenge mode grades
freehand drawings in the student's own vocabulary, and a course can walk
from the first scroll to the full taxonomy on a graded corpus.

The questions, gathered: rule on the two divergences; judge the Agonothetes
— faithful completion or an addition Peirce's text will not bear; say
whether the bridge we draw between the inner evaluation game and the outer
transformation game (de-iteration as the semantic mapping) stands correctly;
and tell us what would make this genuinely useful to the scholars, teachers,
and students of this logic. A competent refutation would count as the game
working. No one has the last word — which we take to be your tradition's
point as much as ours.

With respect and gratitude,

Michael Hauan
